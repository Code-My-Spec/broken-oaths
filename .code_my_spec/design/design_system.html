Broken Oaths — Design System

A browser-first, asynchronous 4X on a hex globe. The audience is Gem-Weary Wes: a mid-30s-to-50s casual strategy player who plays in 2–10 minute work-break sessions, in a browser tab, on whatever machine is in front of him. Every choice below is answerable to that: legible at a glance, quiet enough for a work laptop, and honest about state so he can quit mid-turn without losing his place.

Theme

A single custom daisyUI 5 theme named oath, applied dark-only across the entire app — marketing, auth, world picker, and game board. There is no light variant and no theme toggle in production.

Why custom, not a built-in

The board is already a fixed deep-space backdrop (#04060d). A built-in theme either fights it (coffee is warm-brown against cold black) or flattens the game into an admin console (dim, night). The oath palette is tuned so panel chrome recedes into the same void the globe hangs in, and so the one warm colour on screen — seal gold — is always the thing you can act on.

Why dark-only

Wes plays in a tab at work. A dark UI reads as a terminal from three feet away, which is the discretion this audience actually wants. It also halves the surface: one palette to maintain, one pass of QA per panel, and no hard cut in brightness when he enters a world. Space has no light-mode answer, so the board would have stayed dark regardless.

Theme against the board

The chrome sits on the space backdrop, never beside it. base-300 is the darkest step so floating panels read as a layer above the void rather than a hole punched in it.

Colour is never the only signal

Roughly 1 in 12 men has a red/green deficiency, and this persona skews male and 35+. Every status colour is paired with an icon or a word — never a bare coloured dot. On the globe, player ownership uses hex borders plus a name label, not fill hue alone.

Reserved: gold is not a currency colour

Seal gold means "you can do this now". It must never be attached to a premium currency, a timer skip, or an upsell — the genre trained Wes to read gold sparkle as a paywall, and Broken Oaths has no paywall to advertise. Resources render as labelled numbers with icons, in base-content.

Typography

No webfont on the critical path. Text paints on the first frame, which is the entire promise of "a URL is the whole install." Two stacks: a system sans for everything readable, and a monospace with tabular figures for everything countable.

Sans — all UI and prose

--font-sans

system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI",
Roboto, Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif

Renders as San Francisco on macOS/iOS, Segoe UI on Windows, Roboto on Android. Native everywhere Wes plays, zero bytes, no FOUT.

Mono — every number

--font-mono + font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums

Consolas, "Cascadia Mono", monospace

Resource rates, stock counts, turn numbers, countdowns, coordinates, HP. Tabular figures mean a counter going 9 → 10 doesn't shove the column sideways.

Scale — compact, ratio 1.125, base 16px

A tight ratio so a city panel can nest four heading levels without any of them shouting over the board. Body never drops below 16px — this audience is 35 to 55 and reading a strategy game on a laptop at arm's length.

Marketing overrides the scale on purpose

A 28.8px h1 is right for a HUD and wrong for a landing hero. Marketing and the world picker's page title opt out with explicit sizes (text-5xl / text-6xl). The compact ladder governs in-app chrome; it is not a ban on large type where there is room for it.

Rules

  • Line length caps at ~72 characters. Prose gets a max-w-prose wrapper even inside a wider page.
  • Line height: 1.5–1.6 for prose, 1.15–1.3 for headings, 1.4 for dense panel lists.
  • Weights: 400 body, 600 headings and labels, 700 for the page h1 only. No 300 — thin text on a dark background is unreadable at 45.
  • Never communicate with size alone. A number that turned red is also prefixed with a sign and an icon.
  • 12.6px is the floor. Anything smaller is a design bug, not a density win.

Layout

Two layouts, not one. Everything outside a world is a conventional centered page under a thin top bar. Inside a world, the globe is full-bleed and the app chrome disappears entirely — the HUD floats over the board.

Tone

Broken Oaths should feel like an instrument you consult, not a game that summons you. Wes left Forge of Empires, Elvenar, and three others because they nagged, manufactured urgency, and sold him past their own timers. The interface's entire personality is the refusal to do that.

Voice — plain chronicle

Declarative and unhurried. Archaic in its nouns — oaths, stewards, vassals, ages — and completely modern in its grammar. It reports what happened and what is true. It never tells him to hurry.

Write this

  • The granary is complete.
  • Ashfall will grow in 3 turns.
  • Your alliance took Redmarch.
  • Nothing was lost while you were away.
  • Unrest in Ashfall. A temple would settle it.
  • Not enough production. The walls need 40 more.
  • Wes swore an oath to Marra on turn 38.

Never write this

  • Your city is STARVING!! Act now!
  • Only 3 days left — don't miss out!
  • You haven't played in 6 days.
  • Speed this up instantly for 200 gems.
  • Log in tomorrow to keep your streak!
  • Your alliance is counting on you.
  • Nice job! You're crushing it! 🎉

The rules behind the examples

  • No exclamation marks. Anywhere. Not in flash messages, not in tooltips, not in the tutorial.
  • No second person imperative for optional acts. "A temple would settle it" — not "Build a temple."
  • Never reference his absence. No "welcome back, it's been a while," no decay warnings, no streaks. Time away is not a debt.
  • Countdowns state facts, never pressure. "Grows in 3 turns" is a fact. "Only 3 turns left!" is a threat with no referent.
  • Rejections say what is missing and by how much. An order that fails must name the shortfall as a number he can act on.
  • Never sell. The UI has no surface for a purchase prompt, because there is no purchase. That absence is the product's main promise — do not undercut it with the visual grammar of one.

Motion

  • Motion clarifies, never celebrates. The camera eases to a target so he keeps his bearings. Nothing bursts, sparkles, or confetti-drops on a completed build.
  • Fast and short. 100–200ms for hover and panel state, up to 400ms for camera easing. Nothing he must wait through to act.
  • Nothing loops. A permanently pulsing badge is an attention tax, and this persona is paying too many already.
  • Honor prefers-reduced-motion. Camera easing becomes an instant cut; transitions drop to zero.
  • Muted by default. He is playing in a tab at work. Audio never autoplays.
