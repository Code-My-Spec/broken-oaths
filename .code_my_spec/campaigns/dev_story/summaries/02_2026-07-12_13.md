# July 12-13, 2026: The Globe, and Everything Under It

Fifty-two commits in roughly forty-eight hours, and the first one gives away the method: `98b17c3` banked four research documents (hexasphere geometry, WebGL rendering, CSS-3D feasibility, a map of the existing codebase) and repaired pre-existing test breakage before a single line of new geometry existed. Then, in twenty minutes flat, the whole substrate went in bottom-up: 3D Perlin noise for seamless spherical terrain (`eae07fb`, 23:15), a Goldberg polyhedron GP(f,0) mesh builder with 234 lines of tests (`7272912`, 23:18), orthographic projection (`3bfc62b`, 23:20), fBm terrain over the mesh tiles (`0444a6f`, 23:23), and the flat hex view replaced by a server-rendered globe (`936b815`, 23:28). Five minutes later `8081f8d` deleted the flat world entirely, taking 583 lines and two whole test files with it. No hedging, no compatibility shim.

July 13 was one long argument with the renderer, and the globe lost the same fight four times. Drag-to-rotate and wheel zoom arrived overnight (`9946797`, `eff5cd6`), then an experimental CSS-3D mode that hit 60fps using nothing but HTML divs (`2bdb96f`). Divs did not scale. The next eight hours are a retreat conducted in good order: level-of-detail swapping at the disc edge (`2493342`), coarser far zoom plus no hit-testing while dragging (`6c6409c`), windowing the fine layer around the view center (`3ddbd93`), a baked-texture canvas impostor for far zoom with the coarse hexes deleted (`2190451`), and finally `885f330`, "impostor-first board, vector-canvas near mode, zero DOM tiles." The div approach was abandoned wholesale. `c108d03` wrote the doctrine down one minute later. The scars along the way are real: `90b024d` had to version the `persistent_term` cache keys, `dbf0c5a` taught the hook to survive DOM swaps, and `cde726e` spent over an hour on freezes caused by a "hook/LiveView ownership war." `7ba5459` is the honest epitaph: "phones stop drowning in hex divs."

The clearest documented failure is weather. A drifting cloud layer landed at 20:31 (`db1d41a`), grew storm cells and lightning by 21:00, and `b1bb51d` shipped it with the subject line "about to be rolled back, kept for history." `b8947b5` removed 249 lines five minutes later. When weather returned at 23:36 it came back as an airspace layer of cloud hexes on the same mesh, one shell up (`580c8c6`), which is the lesson the rollback paid for. The last commit of the sprint still trimmed it, dropping the rain animation and slowing the strikes (`3aced7f`).

Between the graphics and the weather sits a two-hour burst of pure platform. CodeMySpec integration with rules, credo checks and spex support (`7938966`), a persona (`ecbc161`), eighteen architecture decision records (`a084847`), then `c728292`: 3,503 insertions of generated accounts, invitations, integrations, authorization and a feedback widget in one commit. Google OAuth, a Kamal fleet deploy kit for prod and UAT, Resend mail, and a TLS fix handing ownership to kamal-proxy followed (`d6ca2df`, `1353ac3`, `c007fdd`, `4d9a5ab`), ending with both environments verified live (`76499b0`).

Two tells about the plan. The sequencing is risk-first: the unproven renderer got the whole day, and the well-understood platform got scaffolded in an evening. And the harness ate its own dogfood painfully, `9f0f151` banking nine framework issues in a markdown file because `create_issue` was broken by a deploy key.
