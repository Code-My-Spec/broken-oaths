# July 16-17, 2026: The World Gets Enemies, and Then It Gets Company

July 15 produced nothing. Not a commit, not a spec. Then the 16th opened at 07:38 and the next 40 hours produced 49 commits, two full story batches, and roughly 20,000 lines of new code and specification. Whatever the gap day was for, it was not idleness.

The morning was cleanup on the city loop from the previous batch. A regression guard went in for the city that used to vanish when its settler completed (`7f31c3d`), and Play's event handlers learned to parse string ids instead of trusting integers off the wire (`cc0b599`). Then came a quieter and more consequential fix: nine city-loop specs were re-anchored to events rather than fixed turn counts (`62534c3`). That lesson had to be relearned three more times over the two days, in `fcb3cb9`, `08d2f1a`, and `0514ef7`. Specs that asserted "after turn 6" were fragile; specs that asserted "when the camp is struck" were not.

The QA harness itself got hardened in the same window. No `mix` invocations at all during QA sessions (`10c7e8f`), and kill the dev server by port, never by process pattern (`1482e0e`), both written as rules after they had presumably cost somebody an afternoon.

At 14:31 the root route was pointed at `/play` (`69a1125`). Seven minutes later that was reversed for a real landing page (`6978c27`). The shortest false start of the period.

Then the real work. Stories 891 through 896 imported at 15:00, five architecture stubs at 16:34, and between 17:11 and 18:02 four commits laid down roughly 7,900 lines of red BDD specs (`67aef38`, `47f4639`, `18afa23`, `31902c8`) covering combat, camp cadence, city defense, garrisons, and the lord. Not one line of combat code existed yet. It arrived at 18:13 (`96c62ce`), followed by camps and real barbarian units (`0a4f56a`), then a BarbarianAI that hunts (`85ca6d3`).

The barbarians immediately misbehaved in exactly the way emergent systems do. Camp placement had to stop stealing claimed tiles (`cff466e`), then obey an 8-15 hex band from QA issue ebe8abf1 (`7f47931`), then hold its spawn counter at cap so kills bought a three-turn grace (`0c355f0`). Three corrective passes across seven hours. The heir schedule also failed to survive a restart, logged as QA issue 0b7e82cd and fixed at 06:00 (`9f8056f`).

That 06:00 timestamp is not a typo. Sprites were drawn at midnight (`db0d278`), wired onto the board at 00:53, and the session ran through to dawn before pausing.

The 17th brought Google sign-in (`43e8fc9`, preceded by the inevitable "supervise the thing you forgot to supervise" fix in `4a8061a`) and a three-act mess around feedback. The hand-rolled widget was first simplified onto a deploy key (`b12b842`, 287 deletions), then deleted outright and replaced by a generated CodeMySpec support widget (`53a4f3c`), which left dead references crashing `/play` until 14:45 (`2128561`).

The day closed with the largest commit of the period: `2543e04`, 148 files and 10,131 insertions, delivering player discovery, known-player panels, per-world chat with blocking and profanity filtering, alliances, cooperative combat with bounty split in proportion to damage dealt, and a `dev_qa_controller` built so the QA agent could drive the game directly. Eight migrations landed in that run, two of them corrections to schema written hours earlier. Batch 4 shipped to UAT and production (`cc50799`), the first prod record in the log. At 23:28 batch 5 was already imported, and at 23:58 Civ 6 tech-tree and resource references went into knowledge. The loop never actually stopped.
