# July 21 to 25, 2026: One Enormous Day, Then the Quiet

The sprint ended the way a sprint actually ends: not with a release, but with one absurd day of work followed by four days of tapering.

Twenty-one of the period's twenty-five commits landed on July 21, starting at 00:13 and finishing at 21:57. The first block ran through the night. `b3cafa6` opened it with the road and terrain movement model plus a playtest UX batch, 25 files and over 1,100 new lines, and the next two hours kept pulling on the same thread. `1bd44bb` inverted the simulation clock so movement resolved every tick while economy resolved every tenth, which required a migration to give worlds their own `economy_turns` and, tellingly, a hex pathfinding reference committed into the project's knowledge directory. `7ae2b9e` then taught interrupted move orders to resume on the following tick, and `fed5df1` put road building behind The Wheel so the new mobility had a research cost.

Two commits in that block were corrections of earlier decisions rather than new ground. `3a9c671` rebuilt coastline generation as the land-adjacent water ring, replacing noise blobs that had looked plausible on a globe and wrong on inspection. `cef65d4` changed center-on-player to use the explored set instead of current sight, because a camera keyed to live vision snapped somewhere useless. Both are small diffs sitting on top of shipped behavior, which is the signature of playtesting rather than planning.

Between 09:02 and 13:09 the game acquired most of its recognizable genre furniture. `15efd86` split archer strength into melee and ranged for Civ 6 congruence, rewriting the combat resolver, camps, siege, and city defense together. `4c3aa65` added Library, Ancient Walls, Barracks, and Water Mill; `7434ca9` added the Pyramids and Hanging Gardens along with a buildable predicate fix found on the way in. `b80bcc6` gave workers the ability to chop woods and rainforest, `3e28896` gave them road-to-destination orders on the back of a new 238-line road builder, and `f5f17a4` added the Scout. Roughly 4,000 lines of feature work in four hours, all of it arriving with tests.

The receipts for how that speed was paid for sit right next to it. `b80bcc6` created a 382-line chop test at 11:12; forty-six minutes later `b3e99ef` rewrote 95 of its lines to make the overflow case growth-agnostic, because the original had been coupled to city growth. `5df82b4` was an explicit simplify pass over the whole batch, net six lines lighter. At 15:46 `80b37dc` fixed a `KeyError` on a city's `player_id` that crashed the LiveView on a worker click, a regression the feature batch had shipped hours earlier. And `8aa867b` closed "the three open PM calls" on Scout garrison, disband, and chop yield, meaning three product decisions had been deliberately deferred to the human and then collected in one pass.

Then the volume falls off a cliff. July 23 produced `6085011`, the empire-wide owner toggle letting stewards set production, complete with a migration, a 194-line gate test, and two new spex files. July 24 produced `6c431f7`, a genuine architectural correction: the world picker had been booting a `WorldServer` as a side effect of listing worlds, and now reads membership and fullness from the database instead. July 25 produced two commits at the same timestamp, `23f4046` easing the globe camera to its target instead of snapping, and `95ad29b` adding `/dev/qa/worlds/:id/reload` to rehydrate a world from the database.

That final pair is not a shipping story. Smoothing the camera and adding a route to reset world state between runs are the things built the day before showing someone the game. The build landed demo-ready, and the work stopped there.
