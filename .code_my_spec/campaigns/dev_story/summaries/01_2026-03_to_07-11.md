# March 19 to July 11, 2026: The Long Quiet Before the Sprint

Broken Oaths began, and then for four months mostly did not continue.

The whole foundation landed in a single evening. On March 19, John scaffolded a Phoenix 1.8 application with authentication already wired (2269035, 68 files, 7,547 lines), and hours later had a Worlds bounded context standing on top of it (d5810f1). That context was the real bet: `noise.ex` for seeded 2D Perlin noise with fractal Brownian motion, `hex_math.ex` for flat-top axial coordinates with cylindrical wrapping, and `generator.ex` layering elevation against moisture to classify eight terrain types. Everything under `Worlds` was deliberately self-contained, with no dependency on the auth system. Nine minutes after the context, the tests arrived: 885 lines across seven files, plus a status doc that honestly listed what was untested and what it "couldn't compile to verify" (e8da327). The merge closed at 19:58 local time, roughly eighty minutes after the initial commit.

Two decisions from that evening are worth flagging, because the sprint would keep one and throw out the other. The pure, deterministic noise and hex math survived intact and became the base of the globe. The rendering did not: hexes were DOM elements shaped with CSS clip-path polygons, panned by buttons and keyboard, and the July sprint's opening act was to replace the flat view entirely (936b815).

Then silence. Zero commits between March 20 and May 31.

The repository woke up on June 1 for something that had nothing to do with the game. In one sixteen-hour stretch, fourteen commits turned it into a market-discovery workspace: an Upwork gather harness with an LLM relevance scorer (ee63890), sweeps pulling 157 and then 223 jobs against migration-intent framings (bb47f6b, da1b395), a competitive teardown and outreach kit, a GEO sweep yielding 216 jobs and a lead list (1dec855), and a delivery SOP concluding that v1 should be a copilot rather than a product (e22ce5d). Not one line of game code. Those working files were swept out on July 13 (c8b13e9), two days into the sprint.

The pace here was exploratory by design. Four months, 18 commits, one evening of actual game engineering, and a repository doing double duty as a scratchpad while a services-versus-product question got settled elsewhere. March established very little surface area, but it established the right primitives: deterministic generation, an isolated context, and tests and status docs written alongside the code rather than after it.
