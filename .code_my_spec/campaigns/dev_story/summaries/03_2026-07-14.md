# July 14, 2026: The Day Broken Oaths Became a Game

Thirty-five commits, 07:50 to 23:37, and by the end of it the globe had cities on it.

The day opened on paperwork. John executed the batch-1 architecture proposal into 16 component specs (`9786fd3`), then immediately filed two more complaints against the harness that had just generated them (`e1700d8`), a habit of critiquing the tool while riding it. He promoted the 3D globe to the default view and left the old DOM board behind a `?mode=classic` flag (`531c988`), then sealed the BDD boundary with a custom Credo check so specs could not reach past the public API (`664dfd5`).

Then the real work. A QA plan and idempotent seed script landed at 10:46 (`9b6ce09`), and in the ten minutes between 11:17 and 11:27 he committed 37 red BDD spec files covering five stories: regions and world picking, turn ticks, movement, fog of war (`5d832e0`, `f48a5da`, `7b9f80b`). Implementation followed in one uninterrupted hour: the GameLive family (`0359d4d`), the Regions partition and the Player/Unit/Order/Exploration schemas (`f2de0d3`), the functional core of Turn, Spawner and Visibility (`fffc5cd`), and finally a 605-line WorldServer that turned all 37 specs green at 12:53 (`b4cacca`). Nearly every spec file was touched again in that last commit, small edits everywhere, the sound of an API discovering its real shape.

The most interesting failure came after everything passed. QA briefs for stories 874, 875 and 876 were filed at 16:15 with screenshot evidence and a clean sweep (`050bfc8`). Fourteen minutes later John tore out the turn model (`fbba3a9`): orders now execute immediately and the turn boundary only recharges movement, replacing the lockstep simultaneous resolution he had built that morning. Four spec files were rewritten with 146 deletions, and their filenames still read `..._boundary_then_everything_moves_at_once_spex.exs`. Seeing the correct thing in a browser told him it was the wrong thing.

Other scars: the Spawner crashed on mountain-enclosed land pockets, found by browser QA and fixed with a regression test (`8417bf5`). Click handling needed a pass to select by exact tile (`d58369f`), then orders needed to path into unexplored terrain (`646a971`), then queued orders needed to keep drawing their remaining path (`7661afd`). The feedback widget had been mounted somewhere LiveView never rendered (`a4bcbf2`). Two copies of the globe renderer had drifted apart until they were extracted into a shared `globe_render.js`, bundled with an optimistic turn guard on the WorldServer (`a8088c7`).

The evening was art. He vendored the Kenney Hexagon Pack as CC0 reference (`dbe9a86`), wrote an `art-gen` CLI against Nano Banana (`cffda31`), drew Aseprite sprites for units and decor (`044c176`), and pattern-filled generated ground textures into the tile polygons (`066f9b3`), which promptly refused to pan with the globe until the patterns were anchored to tile centers (`f77daa7`).

Then he ran the whole lifecycle a second time before bed. Architecture (`41c9892`), canonical Stone Age numbers written down as knowledge (`387a1c2`), 30 red specs for the city loop (`3060fcf`), a 2,892-line functional core of cities, production, yields and growth (`2c52224`), a thatched-hut sprite (`debb1b6`), and the CityPanel UI at 23:37 (`c162089`). Roughly 13,000 lines of Elixir in a day, and a second complete pass through the harness in the last three hours of it.
