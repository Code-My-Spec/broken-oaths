# Ledger-Minded Mara

Proto-persona (PM self-identification + secondary research), researched
2026-08-21. Decision stake: Broken Oaths' economy/vassalage tuning —
unit and building maintenance costs, tax levers, Produce Wealth (949),
and how aggressively the game should let wide conquest expansion
outrun its own income.

## Role

Highly experienced 4X/strategy player with a deep history in the
Civilization franchise (including Colonization) and real-time strategy
games generally [E1]. Plays to win via military conquest/domination —
not cultural or technological victories. Domination is a real,
recognized playstyle within the genre's own community, if a contested
one: some players "play a lot" of domination mode specifically because
it's the only victory type achievable in any era, while others gravitate
to culture or science [E6]. Predominantly a solo/single-player
strategist, by the genre's own limited multiplayer options as much as
by preference [E1].

## Goals

- **Build the biggest empire through conquest.** Territorial dominance
  is the win condition that matters — not cultural or tech
  accumulation [E1].
- **Compound, don't patch.** When choosing between economic levers in
  a crunch, she picks whichever pays off over time — growing income or
  production — over whatever fixes the immediate problem fastest [E1].

## Pain Points

- **Unit maintenance outrunning income during rapid expansion** — but
  she explicitly frames this as a *good* mechanic, not a bug to be
  fixed. Unit upkeep genuinely does become the largest recurring
  expense for an overextended empire in the genre's own economy model,
  which is exactly why the tension reads as real rather than
  artificial [E1, E2].
- **The actual pain isn't the tension — it's when every available
  lever is bad.** Disbanding costs sunk investment in the veteran
  units conquest depends on [E1, E3]. Converting production to gold
  (this project's Produce Wealth, 949 — "build merchants" in
  Civilization) is a bad trade: roughly a 4:1 exchange rate against
  spending that production directly, and it stunts the city growth
  that "biggest empire" actually depends on [E1, E5].

## Context

- Solo/single-player by experience; plans around the tick/turn economy
  proactively rather than reacting to it — she tries to grow income
  and production *ahead of* a crunch, not after going negative [E1].
- Comes from a genre where forced consequences for negative gold
  (auto-disbanding units, science penalties) are an established,
  actively-discussed mechanic players manage carefully rather than a
  rare edge case [E3].

## Decision Drivers

Hierarchy, most to least preferred, all screened by "does this
compound long-term" rather than "does this fix the problem fastest":

1. **Grow income/production ahead of time** — the proactive, preferred
   move [E1].
2. **Raise taxes** — an acceptable, reversible lever [E1].
3. **Accept the squeeze and slow down** — tolerable; the tension itself
   is fine [E1].
4. **Disband units** — last resort; sacrifices conquest capability and
   sunk cost in veteran units [E1, E3].
5. **Convert production to gold** — least preferred; explicit
   opportunity cost against city growth, not a neutral lever [E1, E5].

## Quotes

- "It's really just a good mechanic... when you over expand and you
  run out of money because you were too aggressive in your expansion,
  which is kind of a feature, not a bug." [E1]
- "Disbanding units is always a last resort because you wanna conquer
  people and you want to keep your high-level unit[s]." [E1]
- "[The lever I pick is] the thing that gives you... the best
  long-term benefit — building Gold instead of buildings and units...
  keeps my cities from getting bigger and better." [E1]

## Anti-Patterns

Design traps that break this persona:

- **Removing or softening the overextension/gold-crunch tension
  entirely.** She wants it kept as a meaningful mechanic, not solved
  away [E1, E4].
- **Making "convert production to gold" the path of least resistance**
  out of a gold crisis. She already treats it as a bad trade [E1, E5];
  a design that nudges players toward it as the easy fix would read as
  wrong to her.
- **No proactive lever.** If the only response to a coming crunch is
  reactive (disband, wait it out), it removes the playstyle she
  actually wants — planning income and production ahead of the
  squeeze [E1].
